## Bulk Shipments: More Things That Can Go Wrong

Shipping cargo in bulk — whether that's dozens of boxes, several pallets, or a full truckload — carries a different level of complexity than smaller shipments. The bigger the volume, the more there is to prepare.

This checklist is built on years of experience handling B2B shipments on the Jakarta-Bandung route. Use it as your guide before every large shipment.

---

## Part 1: Preparing Your Goods (2 Days to 1 Day Before Shipping)

### Inventory and Counting

- [ ] Count units/pallets/boxes accurately
- [ ] Weigh the total actual weight
- [ ] Measure total or per-unit dimensions (length x width x height)
- [ ] Record the volumetric weight: (L x W x H) / 4,000 (in cm → kg)
- [ ] Use whichever is greater between actual weight and volumetric weight for your cost estimate

### Condition of Goods

- [ ] Inspect the condition of your goods — anything damaged before shipping?
- [ ] Photograph the condition of your goods before packing
- [ ] Separate goods that need special handling (fragile, liquid, flammable)
- [ ] Make sure goods that shouldn't come into contact with each other are packaged separately

### Packing

- [ ] All boxes use double-wall cardboard (not single-wall)
- [ ] Individual items have protective material (bubble wrap, foam) for fragile goods
- [ ] Empty space inside boxes is filled with cushioning material
- [ ] All boxes are sealed and reinforced with packing tape
- [ ] Pallets are stacked neatly and stabilized with stretch wrap
- [ ] Heavy items at the bottom, lighter items on top
- [ ] Nothing overhangs the edge of the pallet

---

## Part 2: Shipping Documents

- [ ] **Delivery Note (Surat Jalan)** — prepared with full details: sender, recipient, contents, quantity
- [ ] **Packing List** — a complete list of every item in the shipment
- [ ] **Invoice** (if required) — for accounting and recipient verification purposes
- [ ] **Purchase Order** — the recipient's PO number, if applicable
- [ ] All documents printed in sufficient copies (usually 2-3 sets)
- [ ] One set of documents prepared for the driver, one set kept in the sender's records

### Package Labeling

- [ ] Every box/pallet has a destination label: recipient's name, full address, phone number
- [ ] Sender label attached
- [ ] Handling labels in place: FRAGILE, THIS SIDE UP, DO NOT STACK (as needed)
- [ ] Boxes numbered: "1 of 20," "2 of 20," etc., for easy counting

---

## Part 3: Coordinating With Your Logistics Partner

- [ ] Pickup schedule confirmed (1 day ahead or earlier)
- [ ] Required truck size confirmed (single-axle / tronton / container)
- [ ] Total weight and dimensions provided to your logistics partner for rate calculation
- [ ] Special instructions communicated (fragile handling, unloading order for multiple destinations)
- [ ] Departure schedule and estimated arrival agreed
- [ ] Driver's contact number received

---

## Part 4: Preparing the Warehouse / Pickup Location

- [ ] Loading/staging area cleaned and ready
- [ ] Forklift or loading equipment available and in working order (if needed)
- [ ] Personnel responsible for loading assigned
- [ ] Truck access to the warehouse area is unobstructed
- [ ] Gate/security operating hours communicated to the driver
- [ ] Site access procedures (for industrial estates) prepared: access cards, cover letters

---

## Part 5: Coordinating With the Recipient in Bandung (or Jakarta, on the Return Leg)

- [ ] Recipient notified of the estimated arrival schedule
- [ ] Recipient confirms an unloading area is available
- [ ] Recipient's contact number provided to the driver
- [ ] Sign-off procedure for receiving goods communicated
- [ ] If there are multiple delivery points, the order and schedule for each confirmed

---

## Part 6: When the Driver Arrives for Pickup

- [ ] Match shipping documents against the physical goods
- [ ] Count boxes/pallets together with the driver — both parties agree on the count
- [ ] Driver signs the goods receipt
- [ ] Photograph the condition of goods during loading (documentation)
- [ ] Confirm goods are loaded correctly and secured inside the truck
- [ ] Get an estimated arrival time from the driver

---

## Part 7: Monitoring in Transit

- [ ] Keep the driver's phone number on hand
- [ ] Track shipment status against the estimate (if there's no update by the estimated arrival time, contact the driver)
- [ ] Confirm to the recipient that the shipment is on its way
- [ ] If there's a delay, inform the recipient immediately

---

## Part 8: Confirming Receipt

- [ ] Recipient confirms the goods have arrived
- [ ] Quantity check: does the amount received match the amount shipped?
- [ ] Condition check: any damage?
- [ ] Delivery note signed by the recipient
- [ ] Signed copy of the delivery note returned to the sender (for records and reconciliation)
- [ ] If there's a discrepancy or damage, document it and report it to your logistics partner right away

---

## Extra Tips for Frequent Bulk Shipments

If you ship in bulk on a regular basis, consider:

**Create standard document templates** — delivery notes, packing lists, and labels you can reuse instead of building from scratch every time.

**Save this checklist as a template** and make it your warehouse team's SOP — consistency is the key to shipments that consistently run smoothly.

**Evaluate every shipment** — once a shipment is complete, note what went well and what could be improved. Small, consistent improvements add up to a far more efficient system over time.
