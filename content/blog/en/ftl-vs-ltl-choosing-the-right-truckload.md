## What Are FTL and LTL?

When talking with a freight or cargo service, two terms come up often: **FTL (Full Truck Load)** and **LTL (Less Than Truck Load)**. Understanding the difference is key to optimizing your business's logistics costs.

### Full Truck Load (FTL)

FTL means you rent **one entire truck** to carry only your goods. The truck's full capacity — both weight and volume — is dedicated exclusively to your shipment.

**FTL characteristics:**
- The truck travels directly from your pickup location to the destination
- No loading or unloading stops along the way
- Cost is calculated per trip (not per kilogram)
- Suited to loads above roughly 3 tons or large volumes

### Less Than Truck Load (LTL)

LTL means your cargo is **combined with other shipments** in the same truck. You only pay for the space you use.

**LTL characteristics:**
- Multiple shippers share one truck's capacity
- Lower cost per shipment
- The process may involve a consolidation point
- Suited to loads below a truck's full capacity

## FTL vs LTL Comparison

| Aspect | FTL | LTL |
|---|---|---|
| Cost per shipment | Higher | Lower |
| Cost per kilogram | Can be cheaper if the load is full | More expensive per kg |
| Speed | Faster (direct) | Can take longer (consolidation stops) |
| Cargo safety | Safer (not mixed with other loads) | Some risk from additional handling |
| Schedule flexibility | More flexible | Depends on consolidation schedule |
| Ideal for | Large/heavy loads | Small-to-medium loads |

## When to Choose FTL

### When Your Load Is Large

If your cargo volume already fills more than 50-60% of a truck's capacity, it's usually more economical to rent the whole truck (FTL). What you'd pay for "empty space" doesn't outweigh what you gain from FTL.

### When Your Goods Are Sensitive or High-Value

For goods that need special handling — expensive electronics, precision equipment, or fragile items — FTL is the better recommendation. Your cargo stays in one place, isn't mixed with other loads, and carries a lower risk of damage.

### When the Deadline Is Tight

FTL travels directly from pickup to destination with no stops along the way. If you need shipping that's predictable and fast — for example, to meet a factory production deadline — FTL is the safer choice.

### When Your Goods Need Special Conditions

If the truck needs to be specially set up for your cargo (a specific temperature, orientation, or ventilation requirement), FTL is the only sensible option.

## When to Choose LTL

### When Your Load Is Small or Irregular

If your shipping volume is inconsistent or too small to fill a truck, LTL is far more cost-efficient.

### When Your Logistics Budget Is Limited

For growing businesses or low-frequency shipments, LTL lets you access professional freight service at a more affordable cost.

### When Your Goods Aren't Particularly Sensitive

For durable goods that don't require special storage conditions, LTL is a perfectly fine, economical choice.

### When There's No Tight Time Constraint

If you're not tied to a very tight deadline, LTL's more flexible schedule can be the best choice for cost efficiency.

## A Simple Cost Example for the Jakarta-Bandung Route

Let's compare with a few concrete scenarios:

**Scenario A: You're shipping 1,000 kg of electronics**

- LTL: Cost per kg × 1,000 kg = cheaper per shipment, but higher handling risk
- FTL: Rent a small truck outright = higher cost, but cargo stays safe, direct, and faster

For high-value electronics → **FTL is the better recommendation**

**Scenario B: You're shipping 500 kg of non-fragile raw materials twice a week, regularly**

- LTL: More cost-effective, and the schedule can align with your regular shipments
- FTL: Less efficient, since the volume isn't enough to fill a truck

For regular, small-volume, non-fragile shipments → **LTL is more efficient**

**Scenario C: You're shipping 8,000 kg of finished products to a distributor in Bandung**

- FTL: Rent a large truck, travel direct, efficient for large volumes
- LTL: Not practical at this volume

For bulk shipments → **FTL is clearly the right call**
